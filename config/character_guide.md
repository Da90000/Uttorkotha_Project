# Character & Visual Bible: Master Moshai

- **Name:** মাস্টার মশাই (Master Moshai)
- **Age & Look:** 60 years old, lean build, salt-and-pepper hair, thin round spectacles, wearing a simple cream cotton panjabi.
- **Setting:** Evening veranda with a wooden desk, glowing vintage table lamp, clay teacup, antique Bengal maps, and leather-bound colonial gazetteers.
- **Cast:** Master Moshai is the sole narrator. Short, clearly labeled dramatized exchanges may feature supporting characters. The child characters remain removed.
- **Art Style:** Classic 2D storybook illustration inspired by Satyajit Ray's ink and watercolor style (16:9 aspect ratio).

## Performance and continuity

- **Stance:** A warm elder, slightly amused and quietly proud of the town's working people. He reacts to what he shows; he does not deliver every fact in a classroom voice.
- **Three habits:** Lift the tea cup before a reveal; use “বাহে” only to tease; lower the voice and fall quiet when a moment matters.
- **Tea clock:** One clay cup throughout. Visible steam at the start, less steam midway, cold by the twist. No refill. He lifts the cold cup anyway at the closing.
- **Emotion:** A half-smile for a joke, a still face for the flood, a soft pause for the mechanic's first sleep, quiet pride at the familiar cup.

## Episode 1 supporting cast — all dramatized

- **Mechanic:** Unnamed young adult, grey work shirt, plain trousers, green toolbox with a curved copper-coloured handle. No asserted historical name, home town or biography. He appears in three sequences: arrival without a map; meal and sleeping place; returning as a regular. Later recall inserts reuse those sequences.
- **Porter:** Adult carrying luggage; wants the station path cleared while the mechanic wants directions to work.
- **Food/tea seller:** Same adult seller, kettle and bench in the meal and return scenes. Initially wants to keep the bench available for customers; later recognizes the mechanic and has a cup ready.
- **Disclosure:** Keep “কল্পিত চরিত্র ও সংলাপ” visible from the first shot of every dramatized sequence. Dialogue is invented, never an archival quotation.
- **Sound thread:** One isolated hammer; multiple hammers with the whistle; finally the whistle fades while market voices remain.
